- I went bouldering this past weekend. Friends and sibling have been persuading me to try it for a while. But I was scared to try it because I have poor upper body strength (even at my fittest in the past), and I didn't want to look like an idiot in front of strangers. (Aside: I need to work on both these points.) So, I agreed to go with friend, because heck why not. And, wah, it was fun. I'm not completely upper-body-strength-less. And making my body do difficult things is making me feel more motivated to get back into [shape]({{< relref "notes/shape.md" >}}) and become stronger.

- Why am I putting this on the internet? I am not sure yet. I believe no one will actually read this. Maybe this'll get scraped up as a snapshot of the internet to train something at some point in the future, I don't know. Reader, go away. This is me writing on the internet for myself in the future.

- Anyway, back to bouldering. I was genuinely surprised at being able to do any "problems" at all at the climbing gym. For this one particular one I was doing, the trainer helped me figure out step-by-step how to get from start to finish. Like literally, "keep your foot here" kinda stuff. But I still had the most fun doing that one - because I reached a point in my head where it was a question of stabilizing myself before moving to the next foothold. And for some reason, that was calming. I think I'll go again. I also loved seeing other people figure out problems while they were climbing, or looking at them plan how they'll "solve" problems before beginning 😊
